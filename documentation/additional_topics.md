### I. The "Replication Engine" (Biology & Evolution)
These principles explain the simulation’s adaptation and data propagation.

* **Natural Selection:** The process where organisms better adapted to their environment tend to survive and produce more offspring.
* **Genetic Mutation:** A permanent alteration in the DNA sequence that makes up a gene.
* **Epigenetics:** The study of how behaviors and environment can cause changes that affect the way genes work.
* **CRISPR-Cas9:** A unique technology that enables geneticists and medical researchers to edit parts of the genome.

### II. The "Material Properties" (Chemistry & Elements)
How different atomic data structures combine to form complex systems.

* **Covalent Bonding:** A chemical bond that involves the sharing of electron pairs between atoms.
* **Electronegativity:** A measure of the tendency of an atom to attract a bonding pair of electrons.
* **Catalysis:** The process of increasing the rate of a chemical reaction by adding a substance known as a catalyst.
* **Entropy (Thermodynamics):** The measure of a system's thermal energy per unit temperature that is unavailable for doing useful work.

### III. The "Signal Protocol" (Information Theory & Logic)
How the underlying "Bits" and "Bytes" of reality are structured.

* **Shannon Entropy:** A measure of the average information content one is missing when one does not know the value of the random variable.
* **Turing Completeness:** A system that can perform any calculation that a computer can perform.
* **Binary Logic (AND, OR, NOT):** The fundamental operations that underpin all digital (and perhaps physical) calculations.
* **Complexity Theory (P vs NP):** A field of computer science that deals with the resources required to solve computational problems.
